**System One: Install Call Tracking (Cost: $50-$150/month)**

[VISUAL: Screen recording of CallRail dashboard]

Use CallRail, CallTrackingMetrics, or if you're already using Lawmatics, use their built-in call tracking.

You install this, you'll probably find the same thing. It's painful. But you can't fix it until you see it.

**System Two: After-Hours Coverage (Cost: $200-$800/month)**

[VISUAL: Split screen showing in-house receptionist vs after-hours service]

You have three options here.

Option A: Answering service. A real human answers, takes basic info, sends you a text. Ruby Receptionists, MAP Communications, Alert Communications — they all do legal intake. Costs about two to four hundred a month.

Not perfect. They're not going to close the case for you. But they'll capture the lead and you can call back in the morning.

Option B: AI intake. Tools like Clio's intake bot or Smith.ai. They text the caller immediately, ask qualifying questions, book a consultation. Works better than you'd think for simple intake.

Costs about the same as a human service, sometimes less.

Option C: Overflow to a paralegal's cell phone after hours. This is what we set up for Mandall Law. After 6 PM, calls overflow to a team member who's on call. They get paid a stipend. They answer.

[PAUSE]

Any of these options costs less than one signed PI case. If you're spending more than two grand a month on ads and you don't have after-hours coverage, you're leaving five figures on the table every year.

**System Three: Speed-to-Lead System (Cost: Time + Training)**

[VISUAL: Stopwatch graphic]

Here's the rule: answer in under ten seconds. Not fifteen. Not twenty. Ten.

How do you do that?

First, your receptionist or intake coordinator needs to know that answering the phone is priority one. Not finishing the email. Not wrapping up the other call perfectly. Answer the phone.

[PAUSE]

Second, if your receptionist is on another call, you need call overflow. Either to another team member, or to an answering service, or to voicemail with an immediate text back.

Third, track it. Every week, pull your call tracking report. Look at average ring time. If it's above fifteen seconds, you have a problem.

[CUT TO: Host on camera]

One firm we worked with had a receptionist who was also doing billing and scheduling and about nine other things. Average ring time: thirty-four seconds. They hired a second person just for intake. Ring time dropped to eight seconds. Signed cases went up 40% in two months.

Same ad spend. Same traffic. They just answered the damn phone faster.
