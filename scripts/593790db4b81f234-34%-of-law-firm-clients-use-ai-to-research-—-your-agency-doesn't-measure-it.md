## CONTENT (6-8 minutes)

**[CUT TO: Speaking head]**

[VISUAL: Title card — "THE 4-LAYER TRACKING SYSTEM"]

### Layer 1: Source Attribution — Know Where Every Call Came From

**[VISUAL: CallRail or similar dashboard]**

Most law firms use one phone number everywhere.

Website, Google Ads, business card, directory listings — all the same number.

You have no idea what's working.

[PAUSE]

Here's what you need:

Dynamic number insertion for your website. Different number for every traffic source.

Someone hits your site from Google Ads? They see (555) 100-0001.
Organic search? (555) 100-0002.
Came from ChatGPT clicking a link in a citation? (555) 100-0003.

[VISUAL: Diagram showing how dynamic numbers work]

This costs about $200 a month through CallRail or similar tools.

Your agency should have set this up on day one. If they didn't, that's a red flag.

### Layer 2: Lead Quality Scoring — Not All Calls Are Equal

**[VISUAL: Spreadsheet showing call quality data]**

You get 60 calls in a month. Cool.

How many were:
- Wrong number
- Existing client
- Not your practice area
- Too far away
- Can't afford you

[PAUSE]

We had a criminal defense firm getting 80 calls a month from Google Ads.

Sounds great, right?

[VISUAL: Pie chart breaking down call types]

39 were existing clients calling the main line.
11 were people looking for family law — wrong practice area.
8 were in a different state.
6 were spam or robocalls.

That's 64 out of 80 calls — 80% — that were never going to sign.

Actual qualified leads? 16.

At $120 per call from Google Ads, they were paying $9,600 a month for 16 qualified conversations.

[PAUSE]

That's $600 per qualified lead.

Their agency was reporting "80 leads" and celebrating.

**[VISUAL: Screen recording of call listening session]**

Layer two means you listen to calls. Tag them. Score them.

- Geographic fit: yes/no
- Practice area fit: yes/no
- Urgency: high/medium/low
- Budget indicators: can they afford your retainer?

Your CRM should have a field for "lead source" and "lead quality."

If your agency can't show you this data, they're not doing their job.

Thirty-four percent of people — according to legal industry surveys from Q4 2024 — are now using AI tools to research lawyers before they search Google.

"How did you hear about us?"

Options:
- Google search
- Social media
- ChatGPT or AI tool
- Referral from [name]
- Saw an ad
- Other

[VISUAL: Survey results showing 11 people selected "ChatGPT or AI tool"]

When that PI firm we worked with started asking this, 11 people in one month said they found the firm through an AI tool.

We cross-referenced that with their call tracking data.

Those 11 calls had a 27% conversion rate — triple their Google Ads rate.

And their agency wasn't optimizing for it at all.

[VISUAL: Screenshot of law firm's online profiles]

Here's what we changed:

1. Updated their profiles on directories AI tools scrape — Avvo, Justia, Martindale-Hubbell
2. Added structured data to their website so AI can parse their practice areas
3. Built out detailed attorney bios with case results — AI loves specifics
4. Created a dedicated page answering "How do I choose a [practice area] lawyer in [city]"

[PAUSE]

Two months later, AI-attributed calls went from 11 to 19.

Same ad budget. Better targeting for how people actually search now.

### Layer 4: Case Outcome Tracking — Revenue, Not Vanity

**[VISUAL: Spreadsheet showing revenue per source]**

The final layer: did they sign?

[PAUSE]

Most agencies stop at "we got you 40 leads."

Cool. How many signed? What was the case value?

[VISUAL: Example tracking sheet]

Here's what you track for EVERY lead:

- Source (Google Ads, SEO, AI, referral)
- Date of first contact
- Date of consultation (if they scheduled one)
- Did they show up?
- Did they sign?
- Case value or retainer collected
- Close date

They were spending 60% of their budget on Google Ads — the lowest ROI channel.

[PAUSE]

We shifted budget into SEO and optimizing for AI discovery.

Same total spend. Revenue up 38% in four months.
