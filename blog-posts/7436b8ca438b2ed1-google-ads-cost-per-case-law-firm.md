Plus another $12,000 in agency fees.

That's $7,111 per signed case. Just in ad costs.

Let's break down what actually went wrong here, what good numbers look like, and how to fix it before you waste another $5,000.

Because buried in those 47 "leads" were:

Of those 16 real prospects, the intake team reached 8 of them. And signed 2.

That's the real conversion path. Not clicks. Not "leads." **Signed cases.**

The agency optimized the campaigns for clicks and form fills. They got paid whether those leads turned into cases or not. Classic misaligned incentives.

This is the question every law firm owner asks. And the answer actually isn't "it depends."

Here are real benchmarks from personal injury firms running profitable Google Ads campaigns:

**Personal Injury (car accidents, slip and fall):**
- Cost per click: $150-$300 in competitive markets
- Cost per lead (actual prospect): $400-$800

Spending $7,111 per case when your revenue per case is $25,000? That's a 71% margin. Still profitable — barely — but you're leaving money on the table.

- 8-12 actual prospects (not junk leads)

## The 5 Reasons Your Google Ads Cost Per Case Is Too High

After auditing 47 law firm Google Ads accounts, we see the same fatal mistakes over and over:

### 1. Zero Negative Keyword Strategy

The Dallas firm got 12 leads last month looking for criminal defense. Why? Because their campaigns probably bid on keywords like "Dallas injury lawyer" without excluding "criminal," "DUI," "assault," or dozens of other criminal terms.

Each of those useless clicks cost $150-$200. That's $1,800-$2,400 per month burned on traffic that will never convert.

**The fix:** Start with a negative keyword list of at least 100 terms. Every week, review your search terms report and add anything that triggered your ad but isn't your practice area.

### 2. Treating "Leads" and "Prospects" as the Same Thing

Most agencies report "leads" as anyone who fills out a form or calls your number.

But a lead from someone already represented by another firm isn't a lead. It's noise.

**The real funnel looks like this:**

- 100 clicks → 15 form fills/calls (15% conversion)
- 15 form fills/calls → 8 actual prospects (53% qualified)
- 8 prospects → 3 reach intake (37% contact rate)
- 3 intake conversations → 1 signed case (33% close rate)

If your agency is reporting on step 1 and 2 but ignoring steps 3 and 4, they're measuring the wrong thing.

**The fix:** Implement call tracking (CallRail costs $45/month) and tag every lead as "qualified" or "junk" in your CRM. Force your agency to report on cost per *qualified* lead, not total leads.

### 3. No Geographic Precision

The Dallas firm mentioned their agency "ran ads to the wrong zip code for 4 months."

This happens more than you think. An agency sets the campaign to target "Dallas metro area" and Google interprets that as a 50-mile radius. Suddenly you're paying $200/click for someone in Fort Worth who will never hire a Dallas attorney.

**The fix:** Go into your Google Ads account → Settings → Locations → Advanced Search. Verify the exact zip codes you're targeting. If you only take cases in Dallas County, exclude Tarrant, Collin, and Denton counties.

### 4. Landing Pages That Don't Convert

Your ad gets clicked. The prospect lands on your homepage. They see a generic hero image, a paragraph about "experienced representation," and a contact form.

They leave.

Here's what a PI landing page needs:

- Headline that matches the ad copy exactly ("Dallas Car Accident Lawyer")
- One clear call to action above the fold (phone number + click-to-call button)
- 3-5 bullet points showing case results or settlements
- Video of attorney (30-60 seconds, just talking to camera)
- FAQ section addressing top 3 objections ("Do I pay upfront?" "What if I lose?")
- Second CTA at bottom

**Conversion rate benchmarks:**
- Bad landing page: 2-5%
- Good landing page: 8-12%
- Great landing page with video: 15-20%

Going from 5% to 15% conversion triples your signed cases without spending another dollar on ads.

### 5. Ignoring Intake Speed

The Dallas firm said their intake team "got 8 on the phone" out of 16 prospects.

That means 8 prospects — people who filled out a form or called — never actually spoke to anyone.

Why? Two reasons:

1. Slow response time (prospect fills out form at 7pm, gets called back at 10am the next day — they've already talked to 3 other firms)
2. No after-hours coverage (prospect calls at 6:30pm on Friday, voicemail, never calls back)

**The data is brutal on this:** Every hour you wait to respond, conversion rate drops 10x. A lead contacted in 5 minutes converts at 9x the rate of a lead contacted in 30 minutes.

**The fix:**

- Set up auto-responder for form fills (immediate text: "Thanks for reaching out, we'll call you in the next 10 minutes")
- Add an after-hours answering service ($200-400/month)
- Track time-to-contact in your CRM and set a team goal of under 5 minutes during business hours

## How to Audit Your Google Ads Account in 20 Minutes

You don't need to be a PPC expert to spot the problems. Here's the checklist:

**Step 1: Check your search terms report**

Google Ads → Campaigns → Search Terms

Look at the last 30 days. Are you seeing searches that aren't your practice area? Add them as negative keywords immediately.

**Step 2: Verify your geographic targeting**

Settings → Locations

Is the radius too wide? Are you excluding adjacent counties where you don't take cases?

**Step 3: Look at device performance**

Campaigns → Settings → Devices

If mobile performance is way worse than desktop (common for PI), adjust your mobile bid adjustment down by 20-30%.

**Step 4: Review landing page conversion rate**

Tools → Conversions

What percentage of people who click your ad actually convert? If it's under 10%, your landing page is the problem, not your ads.

**Step 5: Calculate actual cost per signed case**

Pull total ad spend + management fees for last 3 months. Divide by signed cases from Google Ads in those 3 months. (Make sure your CRM is tagging source correctly.)

If that number is over $5,000 for PI, something is broken.

## Should You Kill Google Ads and Do SEO Instead?

This is what the Dallas firm owner is considering. It's the wrong question.

Here's the real trade-off:

**Google Ads:**
- Results in 30-60 days
- Costs $5K-$10K/month (ad spend + management)
- Scales quickly (double budget = double cases, usually)
- Stops when you stop paying

**SEO:**
- Results in 6-12 months
- Costs $3K-$5K/month (content, links, technical)
- Scales slowly (double investment ≠ double traffic)
- Compounds over time (traffic keeps coming even if you pause)

For a 4-attorney firm, the answer is both.

Run Google Ads to generate cases *today*. Invest in SEO to reduce your cost per case over the next 12 months.

In 18 months, you'll be signing 3-5 cases per month from Google Ads and another 4-6 per month from organic traffic. That's when you're really profitable.
