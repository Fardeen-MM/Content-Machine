## CONTENT SECTION 2: WHAT GOOD ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE (5:00-8:00)

[CUT TO: More conversational, explanatory tone]

Alright. So what *should* this Dallas firm be seeing?

Let me give you real numbers.

[VISUAL: Cost per case breakdown by market]

For personal injury Google Ads in a competitive market like Dallas, a realistic cost per *signed* case — not lead, not click, signed case — is anywhere from fifteen hundred to four thousand dollars.

That range depends on:
- How competitive your specific zip codes are
- What practice areas you're targeting
- How good your intake process is
- What your average case value is

If you're signing car accident cases worth fifty thousand dollars on average, spending three thousand to acquire one is a no-brainer.

If you're signing slip-and-falls worth fifteen thousand, spending four thousand per case means you're losing money.

[PAUSE]

The Dallas firm we're talking about is at fifty-three hundred per case. That's high but not insane — *if* their average case value is forty thousand or more.

But here's what they should be demanding to get that number down:

**Number one: Conversion tracking that goes all the way to signed cases.**

Not form fills. Not calls. Signed cases.

[VISUAL: Diagram showing tracking from click → lead → call → consultation → signed case]

This means your agency needs access to your CRM or case management system. If they're not willing to integrate with Clio or Filevine or whatever you use, they can't actually optimize for outcomes.

Most agencies stop tracking at "lead submitted form." That's worthless.

**Number two: A mature negative keyword list.**

After eight months of running campaigns, this agency should have at least two hundred negative keywords built up.

[VISUAL: Example negative keyword list scrolling]

Things like:
- "pro bono"
- "free consultation" (if you don't offer one)  
- "lawyer salary"  
- City names outside your service area
- Practice areas you don't handle

Every junk lead is a clue. If someone searches "immigration lawyer Dallas" and clicks your PI ad, "immigration" goes on the negative list immediately.

This firm had a sixty-six percent junk rate. I guarantee their negative keyword list has like thirty words on it.

**Number three: Speed-to-lead systems.**

If your intake team can't respond to a lead in under sixty seconds, you need an answering service for overflow.

[VISUAL: Split screen showing missed call vs. immediate answer]

This costs like two hundred dollars a month. That's three percent of this firm's ad budget.

But it would've doubled their contact rate — which means they'd have reached sixteen people instead of eight, and probably signed four cases instead of two.

That's the difference between a thirty-two hundred dollar per case cost and a six thousand dollar cost.

**Number four: Call recording and analysis.**

If you're not recording every call from your ads and reviewing at least ten percent of them, you have no idea why prospects aren't signing.

[PAUSE]

Maybe your intake person sounds bored. Maybe they're not asking about urgency. Maybe they're quoting prices before building value.

You can't fix what you don't measure.

**Number five: A monthly "signed case review" meeting.**

Not a reporting meeting where the agency shows you graphs.

A meeting where you pull up every case that signed, look at where it came from, and work backward.

"Okay, this case came from the 'car accident lawyer near me' campaign. It took three calls to reach them. They signed four days after first contact. Average case value: sixty thousand."

Now you know that campaign is printing money.

Do more of that.

[VISUAL: Example spreadsheet showing case source analysis]

If the agency won't do this meeting, fire them. I'm serious.
